| M1-4 | 2026-08-28 | Host-side rendezvous bounds (plan §B.3 "small accept queue") | Two semaphores in `tor::listener` (`RendezvousLimits`): **32** rendezvous *handshakes* in flight (permit held only across `RendRequest::accept`, itself capped at **30 s**) and 64 *live* circuits; a circuit with no open signaling stream for 30 s is dropped (the request stream owns the tunnel, so dropping it closes the circuit, `tor-hsservice-0.45.0/src/req.rs` 233–241); accepted `OnionStream`s carry a guard that pauses the idle clock; every circuit task lives in the accept loop's `JoinSet` so dropping the listener aborts them. Amended after the second review — *writes* are bounded too: an `OnionStream` write/flush/shutdown with no progress for 30 s fails with `TimedOut` (Arti parks a write while the peer withholds `SENDME` credit, `tor-proto-0.45.0/src/client/reactor/circuit/circhop.rs` 127–137, so every protocol on the stream inherits the bound); `StreamRequest::accept` and `reject` are capped at 10 s; a full accept queue answers `END(DONE)` *before* accepting (tor-hsrproxy's unreachable-target behaviour) instead of `CONNECTED` + `END(MISC)`; `max_concurrent_streams_per_circuit` = 4 and `pow_rend_queue_depth` = 1024. `ping::serve` cuts a peer silent — or not taking its pong — for 30 s, ends every session after 120 s or 100 pings, and reads at most a 22-byte frame (`read_frame_max`, no 64 KiB allocation per announced header); `tornect host` serves at most 64 ping sessions at once. **Amended after the third review — the admission policy** (`tor::Admission`, derived from the built `OnionServiceConfig::enable_pow`, never configurable on its own): with PoW on (`Backpressure`) the accept loop reserves a handshake *and* a circuit permit **before** polling Arti's request stream, so requests beyond the budgets stay in Arti's effort-ordered queue (highest proof-of-work first, oldest first among equals; bounded by `pow_rend_queue_depth`, dropping the lowest effort; expired after `hs_pow_v1_service_intro_timeout` = 300 s; `pow/v1.rs` 782–803, 1169–1177, 1200–1248); with PoW off (`Drop`) the loop drains eagerly and drops what finds no slot, because Arti's PoW-off queue is an unbounded FIFO (`queue_pow_disabled`, 853/1189). The client-side `OnionStream` carries the same 30 s write-stall bound (`shutdown()` on a credit-withholding host cannot hang). `TorNode::publish_with_limits` exists for tests (a one-slot budget in `live_rendezvous_backpressure`); zero budgets are refused | The track-1 cut held one of 8 permits for the whole circuit lifetime with no deadline, so 8 silent rendezvous locked every later client out (security review, high). The first fix bounded silence only: a peer that withheld flow-control credit pinned the pong write, and a peer that kept pinging kept its slot, both for the host's lifetime (second review, high + medium). Now a slot costs its holder one full rendezvous per session budget (≤ 120 s), silent or chatty. The second-review cut polled `next()` unconditionally and dropped the overflow itself: Arti's control loop counts the service as loaded only while its queue is non-empty (`update_suggested_effort`, `pow/v1.rs` 968–1036, every 300 s; a request leaves the queue exactly when the consumer polls, 1254–1272), so the suggested effort stayed at zero, PoW was never demanded and never ordered admission — a low-rate flood decided admission by first-come semaphores (third review, high; verified from the Arti sources). Under backpressure an Arti client whose attempt *fails* retries with effort ≥ 8 (`tor-hsclient-0.45.0/src/pow/v1.rs` 30, 73–80; `connect.rs` 1093) and is dequeued before every no-PoW request; the published effort rises at the next update. **Amended after the fourth review (medium) — that retry is narrower than the third-review text ("a client whose request went unanswered retries") claimed**, verified from the sources: it fires only inside one Arti connect attempt series (`HsPowClient` is created per series from the descriptor's suggested effort, `connect.rs` 929, and the per-service `Data`, 78–125, holds no PoW state), and a request that merely sits in the queue is *unanswered*, not failed, which the Arti client notices only after `rpt_ipt_timeout` (`connect.rs` 1481–1518): three `BuildCircuit { length: 5 }` estimates plus one one-way transit, which its Pareto estimator answers with the 60 s `cbtinitialtimeout` fallback scaled by circuit length — 3 × 150 s + ≈ 40 s ≈ 8 min — until its persisted history holds `cbtmincircs` = 100 build times (`tor-circmgr-0.45.0/src/timeouts/pareto.rs` 524–533, 617–638; `timeouts.rs` 155–172; the nine logged `tornect ping` runs of the evidence note left 55 in `target/tornect-test/h2/state/arti/state/circuit_timeouts.json`). `CONNECT_TIMEOUT` = 60 s bounds the whole `TorNode::connect` (`node.rs` 314–336), the CLI does not retry a `Timeout` (`tor_cmd.rs` 188, 297), and every `connect` uses a fresh isolation group (`node.rs` 311), so Arti starts it from a blank per-service record and a new `HsPowClient` at the descriptor's effort (`state.rs` 561): the effort resets on every call. Tornect's client therefore solves only after a *fast* failure inside one `connect` (the `NOT_RECOGNIZED` retry in `review3-live2.log`), once its estimator is warm enough for the rendezvous wait to end inside the 60 s, or — on its first attempt — once the descriptor's suggested effort is nonzero, which only PoW-bearing arrivals can make it (`num_enqueued_gte_suggested`, `pow/v1.rs` 1150–1158). Under a queue-filling no-PoW flood a cold client's effort-0 request is the youngest of its class — behind every older one, and the first displaced when the 1024-deep queue overflows (`pop_first`, `pow/v1.rs` 1169–1177) — so it times out at effort 0 on every call, for as long as the flood lasts. **Residual exposure, stated**: the listener has no peer identity before the envelope's signed `HELLO`. The tighter choke point is the handshake budget: a request whose rendezvous point is dead or slow holds a permit until Arti's 2 circuit-build attempts give up or the 30 s cap fires, so ≈ 32/30 ≈ 1 dead-RP introduction per second keeps it full (the circuit budget needs ≈ 0.5 rendezvous/s, 64 per 120 s), both far below the 25/s intro rate limit. **Without PoW that is a lockout of every later client** — which is why `tor.pow_defense` defaults on and is refused rather than ignored when it cannot be honoured (M1-5). With PoW the same flood only fills the slots; everything behind them is ordered by effort, so a client that *pays* effort is served before a flood that pays none — the low-rate flood of the third review is fixed (a FIFO wait in Arti's queue, nothing dropped), but whether a client pays is the client's business, and Tornect's own client cannot yet on the unanswered path (fourth review, above); the listener takes what Arti hands it, in effort order, and cannot change that. The cost the host cannot avoid is one circuit build per admitted request (32 × 30 s of handshake budget cycled by the attacker) — paid also for a request whose client stopped waiting while it queued, since a `RendRequest` carries no receive time (`req.rs` 25–47) and the listener cannot skip stale ones; while saturated the loop logs `rendezvous budget full` once per admitted request, the host's only view of the overflow. Per-device accounting, promoting authenticated streams out of the unauthenticated budget (e.g. `OnionStream::mark_authenticated`) and the §B.3 temp-ban list belong to the envelope track, and so does the **client-side follow-up of the fourth review (M1 close)**: when the fetched descriptor advertises `pow-params`, retry inside `TorNode::connect` under a longer overall deadline so that Arti's rendezvous-completion timeout and effort ≥ 8 retry can fire (Arti takes the effort only from the descriptor and its own retries, so this most likely means a longer `CONNECT_TIMEOUT` under PoW), or carry a Tornect-level effort floor across `connect()` calls, which needs an upstream Arti request to persist or accept a client effort. Acceptance: the cold-client step of the VERIFICATION.md "PoW DoS defense" recipe (`beginning solve` logged within `CONNECT_TIMEOUT`) | Permit per accepted stream only (idle circuits would still be free to keep); a single larger semaphore (still a lockout, just bigger); a total-lifetime cap on every circuit inside the listener (would cut legitimate long sessions until authenticated promotion exists); backpressure without PoW (Arti's FIFO would grow without bound); keeping 8 handshakes (a 32-deep honest burst would have waited a full effort-update period for no gain); a configurable admission policy (each policy is only sound with the matching Arti queue) |
